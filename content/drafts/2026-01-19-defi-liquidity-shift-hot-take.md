The GENIUS Act doesn't just regulate stablecoin issuers. It regulates which stablecoins can flow through the US financial system.

By July 2028, digital asset service providers are prohibited from offering non-compliant stablecoins to US persons.

That deadline will reshape DeFi liquidity.

**The split is coming:**

If federal rules define which stablecoins banks, brokers, and custodians can interact with, DeFi protocols face a binary choice: adapt to compliant liquidity or accept shrinking access to institutional capital.

For issuers like Circle, whose USDC already follows most GENIUS Act requirements, the transition is manageable. For algorithmic or lightly-backed stablecoins, it threatens survival.

**Why this matters for protocols:**

Most DeFi liquidity comes from stablecoins. If the dominant stablecoins must meet federal reserve, attestation, and AML requirements, protocols that depend on non-compliant alternatives will see their liquidity pools dry up.

This isn't speculation. It's the predictable consequence of federal stablecoin regulation.

After 25 years watching financial services adapt to new rules, the pattern is clear: compliant infrastructure attracts institutional capital, and institutional capital dominates markets over time.

**The protocols building for compliant liquidity today will capture the institutional flows tomorrow.**

The ones waiting to see what happens? They'll be building while their competitors are scaling.

DeFi's permissionless ethos is about to meet federally-mandated permission structures. The protocols that navigate this tension intelligently will define the next era.
