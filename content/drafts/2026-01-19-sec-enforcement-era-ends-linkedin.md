The SEC dismissed its case against Coinbase.

Read that again. The agency that filed 125 crypto enforcement actions under Gensler—collecting $6 billion in penalties—walked away from one of its highest-profile cases.

Chair Atkins called it the end of "regulation by enforcement."

What's replacing it:
- Innovation exemption policy (coming January 2026)
- Clear rules instead of retroactive investigations
- No-action letters for compliant projects (DTC tokenization pilot already approved)

The SEC is now all-Republican commissioners. The enforcement playbook is being rewritten.

After 25 years in financial services, I've watched regulatory pendulums swing before. The companies that thrive aren't the ones who celebrated or complained—they're the ones who adapted their compliance infrastructure to the new reality.

Clear rules mean higher expectations for those who claim to be compliant.

What's the first compliance process you'd update given this shift?
