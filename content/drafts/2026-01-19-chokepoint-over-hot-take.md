For two years, crypto companies couldn't get bank accounts.

Not because they were doing anything illegal. Because regulators made it clear to banks that crypto clients weren't worth the supervisory scrutiny.

The House Financial Services Committee documented the pattern in November 2025. The joint regulatory statement in January 2023 warning banks about crypto exposure. The March 2023 collapses of Silvergate, SVB, and Signature—the three banks most willing to serve crypto clients.

That era just ended.

In December 2025, the OCC approved five national trust bank charters for crypto firms. These companies can now offer custody, settlement, staking, and stablecoin issuance under federal supervision.

They're not asking banks for permission anymore. They ARE banks.

**What this means for operators:**

The banking bottleneck is gone. But federal supervision brings federal expectations.

State trust company oversight was often lighter touch. National bank examiners are not. Every process, every control, every customer due diligence decision will face scrutiny that many crypto compliance teams haven't experienced.

After 25 years in financial services, I've seen this transition before. When an industry moves from regulatory gray zone to explicit federal oversight, the compliance bar doesn't just rise—it transforms.

The question isn't whether you can get banking access anymore. It's whether your compliance infrastructure can survive examination.

**The companies that built institutional-grade compliance during the drought are positioned to thrive. The ones that treated compliance as a checkbox? They're about to find out what federal supervision actually means.**

The banking desert is over. The compliance marathon just started.
