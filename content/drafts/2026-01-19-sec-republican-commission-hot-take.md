Commissioner Caroline Crenshaw left the SEC last week.

The agency is now all-Republican. Chair Atkins has called digital assets the top policy priority. The enforcement playbook that generated $6 billion in crypto penalties is being rewritten.

**What's actually changing:**

The SEC has abandoned active crypto enforcement actions and issued policy statements on mining, memecoins, staking, and custody. The "innovation exemption" policy—expected by January 2026—will establish clearer paths for compliant projects to operate.

In December, the SEC staff issued a no-action letter for DTC's tokenization pilot. That's the Depository Trust Company—the backbone of US securities settlement—experimenting with blockchain rails under SEC blessing.

This isn't deregulation. It's re-regulation.

**The trap for crypto companies:**

Some will interpret this shift as permission to ignore compliance. That's a mistake.

Clear rules mean higher accountability for those who claim to follow them. When guidance was ambiguous, companies could argue reasonable interpretation. When rules are explicit, violations are unambiguous.

After 25 years in financial services, I've watched this pattern play out across multiple regulatory cycles. The companies that confuse regulatory clarity with regulatory absence get caught when the pendulum swings back.

**Build compliance infrastructure for the rules you have, not the enforcement you're hoping to avoid.**

The SEC isn't going away. It's just changing how it operates. The smart operators are updating their compliance programs to match the new expectations—not celebrating prematurely.
