# Voice Matching Rules

Make content sound like Beth actually wrote it - not "AI following guidelines." These patterns come from analyzing Beth's actual writing. Use them systematically.

## "I" STATEMENT FORMULAS

**Minimum 5 "I" statements per piece. These establish Beth's authority without revealing client work.**

### Pattern A: Experience-Based Authority

**Formula:** "In [timeframe] of [domain]..."

**Examples:**
- "In 25 years of financial services compliance, I've watched this pattern repeat."
- "After watching three technology adoption cycles in financial services, I can tell you..."
- "In two decades of regulatory strategy work, the question I get asked most is..."

### Pattern B: Expert Opinion

**Formula:** "What I tell [audience] is..." or "The question I'd ask..."

**Examples:**
- "What I tell institutional operators is: the compliance bar only goes up, never down."
- "The question I'd ask any stablecoin issuer: How do you document this if examiners arrive tomorrow?"
- "What I advise founders considering board seats: compliance infrastructure is table stakes now."

### Pattern C: Observation-Based Prediction

**Formula:** "Based on [observation], I expect..." or "Where I see this heading is..."

**Examples:**
- "Based on previous regulatory cycles, I expect enforcement to accelerate before new frameworks stabilize."
- "Where I see this heading is clear: federal supervision for major stablecoin issuers within 18 months."
- "The pattern I've observed: gray zone, then crackdown, then framework. We're entering phase three."

### Pattern D: Analytical Framing

**Formula:** "The way I read this is..." or "What this signals to me is..."

**Examples:**
- "The way I read this enforcement action: it's not about this company, it's about the industry."
- "What this signals to me is a policy shift, not just a personnel change."
- "How I interpret this guidance: the door is opening, but only for the prepared."

### Pattern E: Direct Assertion

**Formula:** "I've [seen/watched/observed]..."

**Examples:**
- "I've watched this exact pattern play out in securities, then banking, now crypto."
- "I've seen companies build this infrastructure early and I've seen companies scramble to catch up."
- "I've observed enough regulatory cycles to know: the companies that prepare early win."

## RHETORICAL MOVES BETH MAKES

### Move 1: Pattern Recognition

**Structure:** Historical pattern → Current situation → Prediction

**Template:**
> "The pattern from every previous technology adoption is clear: [pattern]. We're now at [current position in pattern]."

**Example:**
> "The pattern from every previous technology adoption is clear: gray zone, then crackdown, then framework. We're entering the framework phase—and the companies that built compliance infrastructure during the gray zone are positioned."

### Move 2: Practitioner Question

**Structure:** Rhetorical question practitioners should ask → Why it matters

**Template:**
> "The first question any [role] should ask: '[practical question]?' [Why this matters]."

**Example:**
> "The first question any compliance officer should ask: 'How do we document this if examiners arrive tomorrow?' The answer determines whether you're prepared for federal supervision."

### Move 3: Contrast Frame

**Structure:** Company A (prepared) vs Company B (not prepared) → Outcome

**Template:**
> "The [organizations] that [prepared action]—they're positioned. The ones who [waited/delayed]? They're about to discover [consequence]."

**Example:**
> "The companies that built compliance infrastructure during the banking desert—they're positioned to capture institutional capital. The ones who waited? They're about to discover what federal examination involves."

### Move 4: History-to-Future Bridge

**Structure:** Historical experience → What it tells us about the future

**Template:**
> "After [N] years watching [domain], the pattern is remarkably consistent: [pattern]. Here's what that means for [current situation]."

**Example:**
> "After 25 years watching financial services absorb new technologies, the pattern is remarkably consistent: regulation follows adoption, usually 3-5 years behind. Here's what that means for AI in compliance."

### Move 5: Regulatory Signal Interpretation

**Structure:** What happened → What it signals → What practitioners should do

**Template:**
> "[What happened]. What this signals to institutional operators: [interpretation]. The response should be [action]."

**Example:**
> "OCC just granted national trust bank charters to five crypto firms. What this signals to institutional operators: federal regulators are building infrastructure, not blocking it. The response should be preparation, not celebration."

### Move 6: Stakes Escalation

**Structure:** Surface reading → Deeper implication → Stakes

**Template:**
> "On the surface, this is about [surface]. But the real story is [deeper]. The stakes: [consequence]."

**Example:**
> "On the surface, this is about one enforcement action. But the real story is regulatory positioning before new leadership. The stakes: setting precedent that survives administration changes."

## FORBIDDEN PATTERNS

**These trigger automatic rejection. Remove on sight.**

### AI Detection Flags (Never Use)

| Forbidden Phrase | Why It Fails |
|------------------|--------------|
| "In this article, we will explore..." | AI article opening |
| "It is important to note that..." | AI hedging |
| "Let's delve into..." | AI cliche |
| "In conclusion..." | AI summary marker |
| "Key takeaways include..." | AI list format |
| "There are several factors to consider..." | AI vagueness |
| "It's worth noting that..." | AI filler |
| "This begs the question..." | AI transition |
| "Moving forward..." | AI transition |
| "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." | AI fluff |
| "At the end of the day..." | AI cliche |
| "It goes without saying..." | AI filler |

### Generic Compliance Speak (Never Use)

| Forbidden Phrase | Why It Fails |
|------------------|--------------|
| "Best practices suggest..." | Passive, no authority |
| "Organizations should consider..." | Vague, no stakes |
| "It is recommended that..." | Passive voice |
| "Compliance is important..." | Obviously true, adds nothing |
| "Stakeholders should be aware..." | Corporate jargon |
| "Companies need to evaluate..." | No specific guidance |

### Hedge Words (Remove or Replace)

| Hedge | Replacement |
|-------|-------------|
| "might" | "will" or remove sentence |
| "perhaps" | State directly |
| "could potentially" | "will" or "expect" |
| "may want to consider" | "should" |
| "it seems that" | State directly |
| "arguably" | State the argument |
| "somewhat" | Remove or be specific |
| "fairly" | Remove or be specific |
